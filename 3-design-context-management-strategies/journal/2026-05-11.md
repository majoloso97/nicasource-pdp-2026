### Journal
This paper feels foundational for understanding newer AI agents. A lot of what is currently marketed as "agents" can be trimmed down to the core loop described here: reasoning about the next step, executing an action, observing the result, and repeating the process.

What makes ReAct particularly important is that it combines two capabilities that had mostly been explored independently before: chain of thought and interactions with the environment through tools

Instead of reasoning once and producing a final answer immediately, the model updates its reasoning based on observations gathered from external actions. That feedback loop feels closer to how we naturally learn from mistakes (less 'learning', per-se, but the idea stands).

I find interesting how they mention that reasoning works for improving output, yes, but also as a way to keep state. I've noticed that agent harnesses like Opencode, use similar techinques where the trace of reasoning is used to maintain awareness of goals, plans and partial progress across multiple steps, specially when tasks are complex. This makes a lot of sense overall, and again, feels organic.

I got this insight from previous sources, the ReAct paper also mentions it: external tools help to compensate for model weaknesses. Instead of forcing the LLM to 'learn more' during training, these task pieces can be outsourced to other systems, often more reliably.

However, the paper also made me think about risks. Reasoning is useful, but a more complex system inherently brings some 'problems' if not managed correctly: latency can become an issue due to more steps, the overall context is bloated with potentially repeated information from these reasoning and 'back tracking thoughts', broader context could lead to more hallucinations, and overall, larger state means more tokens and more costs.

More complexity makes the system behave more like a black box, which is undesirable in production systems where traceability is essential. Finding the root cause for a certain outcome might become a needle in the haystack type of searc.

Overall, this all comes back to the idea of orchestration, and that the tools given to the AI can 'make or break' the whole system.
